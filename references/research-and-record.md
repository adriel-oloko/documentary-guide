# Research and the record

Stage 4. Do this before writing the script, every time, at both shapes.

The order is not negotiable: research, then the myth table with sources, then the
script. Prose written before the belief is checked launders belief into fact, and
once it is in the script it reads as knowledge.

## Why this comes first

Some subjects arrive with fixed audience belief attached, and most of that belief is
either wrong or unsourced. On this class of film the audience already half knows the
subject, and the half they know is exactly where a laundered fact shows.

Expect three to five corrections per episode. That is the normal yield, not a sign
that something went wrong. The corrections are the payoff: the honest version is
almost always sharper than the myth, and the moment the audience learns they were
wrong is the moment they comment, and comments are one of the strongest ranking
signals there is.

## The research file

`00-research.md`, in the episode folder. Four sections, in this order.

### 1. Myth against record

```
## Myth against record
| Belief | What the record shows | Source |
|---|---|---|
```

Four rows minimum. Every row carries a real source. A row with no source is a rumour
you are about to publish.

Work the rows in this shape:

1. List each claim as the audience holds it, in their words.
2. State what the record actually supports.
3. Where the record is thin, say that it is thin rather than picking a side.
4. Source every line.

### 2. The spine

The mechanism the episode is actually about, in one paragraph. If you cannot write
this paragraph, the episode is a list of facts and it is not ready.

### 3. Numbers worth saying out loud

Every date, dimension, speed, range, toll and count the script will use, each with
the figure written the way it will appear in the narration, as words. This section
is where you catch a number you cannot source before it reaches the script.

### 4. What is genuinely unresolved

The arguments with no agreed answer. The script states both sides rather than picking
the more dramatic account. Genuine disagreement is the strongest material in the
file, not a weakness to be smoothed over.

## The source hierarchy

Highest first. A row is only as good as its position here.

1. **Primary institutional documents.** Official inquiry reports, national archives,
   admiralty and regimental correspondence, coroners and trial findings, audit
   reports, patent filings.
2. **Survey and technical reports.** Wreck surveys, engineering failure analyses,
   classification records, meteorological and hydrological records.
3. **Serious secondary works.** Museum publications, academic monographs, national
   naval and industrial histories.
4. **Contemporaneous press.** Period newspapers and trade journals. Useful, and it
   carries the period's own biases, so treat a claim as a claim.
5. **Popular retellings, documentaries, encyclopedia summaries, forum posts.** Not
   sources. Useful for finding out what the audience believes, which is what the
   left column of the table is for.

A claim that traces to a single popular retelling often enough that naming the
source IS the correction. Say so.

## Rules that are absolute

- **Never invent a source, a date, a dimension, a quote or a toll.** If a number
  cannot be sourced, the number does not go in.
- **Distinguish "the myth is false" from "the myth is true of something else".** A
  real nickname that belonged to a different object is not a lie, it is a
  misattribution, and the distinction is the memorable part of the episode. Write it.
- **Check the brief.** A subject brief supplied by someone else may contain an
  unsupported fact. One real brief named four factories, and one of them does not
  appear in any production table for any variant. Flag it, name the correct one, and
  record that it was flagged rather than repeating it into the script.
- **Some subjects have no hero designer and no single invention moment.** Say so
  explicitly in the research file, as an honesty rule, so that a later edit does not
  invent a lone insight to make the story land. A system, a line or a number can be
  the protagonist, and the episode has to commit to that.
- **Never let a dramatic detail survive on drama alone.** The contested version and
  the agreed version are different claims, and the script says which is which.

## Research at the SHORT shape

Same file, same four sections, fewer rows. Two or three beliefs is enough, and the
spine is one sentence. The hook is the correction rather than the mechanism, because
a minute only has room for one turn.

The one thing you cannot skip at SHORT is the sourcing. A separate source row per
row of the table, even on a sixty second film.

## What the research file is not

It is not in the video. It is not a script draft and it is not published. It is the
evidence file an editor can open in six months when someone challenges a claim on
screen, and it stays in the episode folder for exactly that reason.
