# The topic bank

Eight families. Pick one, not two. Every family below carries an engine that keeps
producing episodes, a place the record actually lives, and a failure mode that has
sunk real channels.

The rule that applies to all eight: **one topic per episode.** Two subjects in one
film is two half films.

---

## 1. Ships and shipwrecks

**The engine.** Naval architecture against the sea. A ship is a design, the sea is a
test, and the gap between them is the story. Works because engineering explains
history better than any other subject class does, and because a ship is a single
object with a fixed, checkable specification.

**Record lives in.** Official inquiry reports, wreck survey reports, naval histories,
museum records, classification society archives, admiralty correspondence.

**Real subjects.** The Vasa (1628), HMS Dreadnought, the Mary Celeste, Project
Azorian and the Glomar Explorer, the Ever Given, the Edmund Fitzgerald, USS
Indianapolis, the Halifax Explosion, the Franklin Expedition, Bismarck and HMS Hood,
the Wilhelm Gustloff, the Andrea Doria, the Hunley, the Antikythera wreck, Brunel's
Great Eastern.

**Failure mode.** Insignia. The most commercially attractive subjects on this lane
are German and Japanese, and no enemy hull may carry any marking, crest, flag, hull
number, name or national symbol. Enemy vessels are written at silhouette and detail
distance only.

**Second failure mode.** Water and scale. Open water renders as a flat plate or a
repeating texture, horizons bend, and a hull at an unknown distance reads as a model.
Every place clause needs an explicit scale anchor and an explicit water behaviour
clause written out in full.

**Shape.** LONG. A mechanism, a sequence and a consequence do not fit in a minute.

## 2. Food

**The engine.** One ingredient or one dish rewriting trade, empire or war. Works
because everybody eats, so the subject is universal, and the mechanism is usually
chemistry or agriculture, which is ad safe.

**Record lives in.** Trade archives, port ledgers, agricultural records, cookbooks,
customs returns, patent filings.

**Real subjects.** Vanilla and the hand pollination problem, ketchup and fish sauce,
the potato and European survival, the tomato and the poison scare, cacao as currency,
ancient honey, heroin cough syrup, the Boston molasses flood, the London beer flood.

**Failure mode.** Complimentary claims. A subject this familiar attracts praise
pieces, and praise pieces flop. The food episode that works carries a paradoxical or
negative claim.

**Second failure mode.** Gimmick drift. A dish that becomes a listicle of facts is no
longer an episode.

**Shape.** Either. A single strange fact is SHORT, a commodity and its consequences is
LONG.

---

## 3. Battles

**The engine.** The plan, the ground, and the people trapped inside it. The plan is
the interesting part. Battles are won by logistics, weather, signals and timing far
more often than by heroism.

**Record lives in.** Official histories, regimental diaries, after action reports,
courts martial, war diaries, letters.

**Real subjects.** The Christmas truce, the Anglo-Zanzibar War, the eclipse that
stopped a battle, the Ottoman siege of Caffa, the Oaken Bucket War, Operation
Mincemeat, bomber pilots, Red Cross nurses, Royal Navy sailors.

**Failure mode.** Ad safety, and this is a monetisation rule rather than a taste
rule. No graphic injury, no bodies in frame, no water full of men, no dramatised
death, no current conflicts. Sequence the episode as design, preparation, action,
aftermath, consequence, and let the machinery carry it. Limited ads cut revenue by 50
to 80 percent, which is the largest avoidable loss on this lane.

**Shape.** LONG if the mechanism carries it. SHORT only for a genuinely odd incident.

---

## 4. Animals

**The engine.** A creature that did something history needed, or refused to. Works
because it is instantly sympathetic, it carries no politics, and the subject is
nearly always a discovery for the audience.

**Record lives in.** Newspaper archives, regimental records, zoological and museum
records, contemporary press.

**Real subjects.** Cher Ami the homing pigeon, Hachiko, Wojtek the soldier bear,
Laika, Owney the mail dog, Sergeant Stubby, Unsinkable Sam, Bosco the mayor, the
Great Emu War, the Pig War, Mrs O'Leary's cow.

**Failure mode.** Sentimentality. The animal is the anchor, not the argument. The
episode still needs a mechanism or a correction, or it is a cute video with a
historical costume on.

**Second failure mode.** Animal cruelty framing. Keep the loss beats implied.

**Shape.** SHORT by default, because the whole weight of these sits on one turn. A
war animal with a theatre of operations behind it can carry LONG.

---

## 5. Machines

**The engine.** One design that made everything before it obsolete. The strongest
version of this is a machine whose success and whose failure are the same property.

**Record lives in.** Patent offices, production tables and variant tables, technical
manuals, ordnance boards, factory records.

**Real subjects.** The M4 Sherman, the mechanical Turk, Velcro and the burr, the
failed glue behind the sticky note, the Slinky, the discovery of X-rays, the
microwave, the heart drug that became something else.

**Failure mode.** Variant drift. A machine anchors better than a face, but only if
the reference plate pins the discriminators in words: hull shape, gun length,
suspension, funnel count. State what the object does NOT have, not only what it does,
because a welded hull will come back riveted if nothing forbids rivets.

**Shape.** LONG. A machine that only needs a minute is an oddity, not a machine.

---

## 6. Disasters

**The engine.** A failure with a cause and a body count. The cause is the episode.

**Record lives in.** Inquiry findings, engineering analyses, coroners reports,
insurance claims, meteorological records.

**Real subjects.** The Halifax Explosion, the Great Smog of London, the Peshtigo
fire, the Galveston hurricane, the Boston molasses flood, the London beer flood, the
Centralia mine fire, the Carrington event.

**Failure mode.** Gratuitous casualty content, which is both an ad risk and a taste
risk. Carry the disaster through instruments, structures, documents and the record's
own voice, and hold the human cost at document distance. A city erased in one
detonation is a legitimate subject; a crowd in the blast is not.

**Shape.** LONG. A disaster is a sequence.

---

## 7. Medicine

**The engine.** The body, the trial, and the mistake. This family has the best
corrections available anywhere, because medicine spent centuries confidently wrong.

**Record lives in.** Journals, trial registries, hospital and ward records, mortality
returns, published correspondence.

**Real subjects.** Semmelweis and hand washing, Jenner and cowpox, the penicillin
mould, John Snow and the pump, Robert Liston the surgeon, the first blood
transfusion, royal haemophilia, the king who was porphyric, ether frolics, the
heart drug that became something else.

**Failure mode.** Presentism. Judging a nineteenth century physician by modern
knowledge makes a smug episode. The interesting question is why the correct answer
was rejected by people who had the evidence.

**Shape.** Either, and the correction is usually the whole SHORT.

---

## 8. Money, heists and forgeries

**The engine.** A fraud, a theft or a crash, and who was holding the loss.

**Record lives in.** Court filings, audit reports, period press, auction records,
provenance files.

**Real subjects.** The Gardner heist, the theft of the Mona Lisa, Salvator Mundi,
the Van Meegeren forgeries, the Isleworth Mona Lisa, the auction of the Roman empire,
Caravaggio on the run, the painting Van Gogh sold once.

**Failure mode.** Admiring the trick. The episode is about the gap between what the
market believed and what was true, not about how clever the criminal was.

**Shape.** SHORT for a single theft, LONG where a market is involved.

---

## Cross family rules

- The failure mode of the family is what breaks the episode. Read it before you
  research, not after.
- A family that has no correction available is the wrong family for that subject. Try
  the same subject through a different family's engine before abandoning it.
- Families combine at the subject level, not the episode level. The Halifax Explosion
  is a disaster and a ship story; you still pick one engine to tell it through.
