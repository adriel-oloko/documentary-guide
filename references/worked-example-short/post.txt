A wounded homing pigeon named Cher Ami saved nearly two hundred American soldiers trapped in the Argonne Forest in October 1918.

About two hundred American soldiers were trapped in a pocket in the Argonne Forest, surrounded by Germans and shelled by their own artillery. Their only way to call for help was a homing pigeon named Cher Ami, so they pinned a message to her leg and threw her into the sky. German fire shot her through the breast, and she fell, then got back up and kept flying. By the time she reached headquarters she had lost an eye and one leg hung by a tendon, but the message arrived and the shelling stopped. Roughly 194 men walked out alive, and the army fitted her with a tiny wooden leg, gave her a medal, and let her retire.
